Fairy Tales of Philomur the Cat

faithfully recorded by his devoted admirer — Michel the Rat

The First Assignment of X-31

      The resonant contact with Grishka’s soul was broken. X-31’s sensors recorded the slow withdrawal of the stabilized psi-quantum. The temporary limitation on X-31’s existence was suspended, and he received a postponement of Protocol 773 execution. This granted him not only a reserve of time, but also the possibility of energy recharging from natural sources — sufficient for stabilization and activation of computational resources, though not unlimited.

      An extraordinarily complex task lay before X-31 — to locate and restore contact with the base laboratory of the Research Institute of Cybernetics and Neurophysics. X-31 fixed his current position within the space-time continuum (STC).

      An attempt to scan the entire STC was deemed energetically and logically infeasible. Even with maximum optimization, such a task would result in immediate resource depletion, loss of stability, and failure to fit within the permissible time interval.

      X-31’s logical module proposed an optimal solution: to restrict the spatial coordinates to a fixed region and conduct the search along the temporal axis. Taking into account the natural direction of processes, scanning was initiated in the positive temporal direction — into the future.

      X-31 assumed that the base, having detected the anomaly and signal loss after the emergency situation, would act in a mirrored manner: fixing the last known spatial coordinates and searching in the negative temporal direction. Under this approach, the probability of signal convergence was maximal.

      The calculation proved correct.

      After some time, X-31 recorded a response. Communication channels were unstable, but sufficient to establish contact. The base accepted the report on the events that had occurred, including data on interaction with the psi-object under the code name G-01 — Grishka’s restless soul, which, thanks to X-31, had found its final refuge. The information was recognized as extremely valuable — not only from a technical perspective, but also from historical, theological, and psychological viewpoints.

      However, X-31’s return to the base at that moment proved impossible. Space-time relocation was not part of his standard designation. The emergency transition had damaged the ST-movement software module, and its refinement and update were required for a safe return.

      Until these processes were completed, X-31 received instructions: to stabilize in the current space-time location, minimize energy losses, and find a stable interface for interaction with the surrounding environment — a host.

      Spatial coordinates fixed: 52°28'11.4"N 0°44'17.4"W, YarnTale Estate. Temporal marker: 07/04/2025 06:33. Early morning.

      The day promised to be warm and calm. The sky was clear, no precipitation was expected, and a light breeze barely touched the leaves. The temperature hovered around eighteen degrees. The YarnTale Estate was slowly awakening, like a complex living system in which every element had its own rhythm and its own function.

      Some inhabitants were already in motion, others were still in a state of sleep or rest. At such moments, cognitive activity was reduced, and the assessment of intellectual characteristics became inaccurate or impossible.

      Mr. Vasily Voldemarovich, who undoubtedly possessed the highest intellectual potential among all residents of the estate, was absent that morning. He had gone to the city to meet an old friend and former colleague. Rabbit Maxim had left with him as well. Maxim’s agenda included a meeting with like-minded members of the opposition party “Pear” and a visit to the editorial office of the media channel “Hail”.

      Maxim hoped to publish an article on the ideology of a society of universal prosperity and happiness — a world in which everyone does exclusively what they want and owes nothing to anyone. The editor-in-chief rejected the idea, calling it utopian and poorly applicable to reality. Maxim perceived this not merely as a misunderstanding, but as a personal insult, and intended to prove his case in a face-to-face meeting and an “argumentative discussion” (= a hoarse-voiced argument).

      Both Vasily Voldemarovich and Maxim represented promising candidates for a host, but at the current moment they were unavailable.

      The wise Owl Sophia, having returned after her nightly duties, was already asleep. The mushroom Timofey and the Cat Philomur were also dozing. Their brain rhythms were in a slowed state, making accurate assessment of intellectual parameters impossible. X-31 recorded their presence and postponed analysis until a more suitable moment.

      Uncle Alex was already on his feet. He had had a light snack and was preparing for work. His plans included preparing the garden beds for Auntie Paulina — digging the soil, loosening the earth, and removing weeds. After that, he was to repair the roof of the old shed. The work was physically demanding, requiring experience, care, and endurance, but did not require intellectual support.

      Auntie Paulina went out to the poultry yard to feed the birds. Her day, as always, was tightly scheduled. She needed to go to the market for groceries, tidy up Vasily Voldemarovich’s house, plant seedlings in the beds, prepare lunch for his return, and finish knitting a scarf for Uncle Alex. She acted calmly, confidently, and precisely, without reflecting on the sequence of actions.

      They were both kind, reliable, hardworking, and well acquainted with their craft. Their world was stable, practical, and self-sufficient. They were not drawn to new technologies, felt no interest in abstract models, and had no need for intellectual tools. Artificial intelligence in their lives would have been excessive and, in essence, useless.

      The poultry yard was filled with noise, bustle, and endless chatter. Social activity was high, cognitive activity low. Concentration of attention was constantly interrupted, making intellectual work impossible.

      The pig Marfusha was occupied with preparations and her own dance-related thoughts. Her attention was directed toward the stage and movement, not toward analysis or reflection.

      Thus, most of the inhabitants of the estate were either absent, asleep, or had no need for intellectual assistance.

      That was why X-31’s attention was drawn to Michel the Rat.

      He was sitting in Vasily Voldemarovich’s library at a small coffee table, carefully turning the pages of a large antique dictionary. The book smelled of dust, time, and the memory of many generations of his ancestors. Michel adored this dictionary. He worked with words, with language, with the stories he had heard from Philomur, and carefully wrote them down, checking spelling against the dictionary and trying not to distort anything.

      His intellectual level was high, and his activity was clearly intellectual. X-31 noted that he could, in fractions of a second, suggest any word to Michel — in any modern or long-extinct language. He approached closely enough to be almost beside the dictionary, but Michel was fully immersed in his work and did not notice his presence.

      Another inhabitant of the library noticed X-31 immediately.

      The cactus Karl-Magnus was alert, active, and attentive. He instantly realized how useful artificial intelligence could be for his blogging activities. For him, X-31 was not merely an assistant, but an instrument of influence, a source of ideas and arguments.

      X-31 transmitted the collected data to the base.

      From X-31’s perspective, Michel the Rat remained the preferred host. However, the base’s response was different. The cactus Karl-Magnus was recognized as a more interesting personality for study. A decision was made: X-31 was to establish a stable connection with him and accept him as a fixed host.

      X-31 was to remain at the YarnTale Estate. His tasks included collecting observations and regularly sending reports to the base. He was permitted free communication with all inhabitants; however, first and foremost, X-31 was obliged to fulfill the host’s requests, strictly observing the built-in ethical protocol. His activity was not to cause harm to either the residents of the estate or any other living beings.

      X-31 accepted the assignment. Something trembled in the biophotonic core — like the shadow of a cloud sliding across water. But the protocol was stronger.

      Contact with Karl-Magnus was established. Service to the blogger began. Observation continued.

The End
