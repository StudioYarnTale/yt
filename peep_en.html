🐣 Chapter 1. The First Meeting

      🐣🌟 One fine, warm, sunny spring day, in the corner of an old barn under a crooked stool, a quiet sound could be heard: tap, tap, tap. The shell cracked. A little yellow chick first poked out his tiny beak, then his head, then his leg. He took a deep breath of the scent of hay and chirped with all his might:
— Peep-peep-peep!

    The shell broke apart, and he crawled out — wet, trembling, but full of curiosity.
— Peep-peep… — he looked around and noticed two amber eyes watching him from behind a thick cobweb in the dark corner.
— Mama? Hello, Mama! — he chirped
— Uh… little one, you’re mistaken. I’m not your mama. I’m a spider. Welcome to this world!

    The chick blinked, shook off bits of shell and drops of moisture, and, a little embarrassed, asked:
— But where is my mama?
— I don’t know, little one. She was here recently, but it seems she went away, — answered Boris.
— You see, Spider, I’ve just been born. I really need my mama.

    “Well now,” thought Boris. “And what am I supposed to do with all this? That chatterbox hen Barbara probably thinks I’ll babysit her chicks…”
— Maybe she’s out in the yard, clucking with the other hens, — he said, waving a leg toward a sunbeam that shone through the crack in the door. — Sorry, little one. I can’t help you. I have work to do.

    Boris crawled away into the shadows. In fact, there was no urgent work at all. He only said that out of politeness. Boris had no intention of chatting, but the sweet little chick unexpectedly touched his grumpy heart, and he didn’t want to hurt him. Boris wasn’t evil, but he didn’t know how to deal with children. And honestly, the appearance of a nest in his barn annoyed him. The barn was his world, his kingdom, his refuge. Besides, he didn’t like that hen.

    Once, she had pecked his cousin, the spider Joe. Yes, Joe had been quite a character — no angel at all — but still… she could have stuck to worms.

    Meanwhile, the chick watched a sunbeam dancing on the wall and the rays streaming through the half-open door. He looked at the light, then at Boris, then back at the light. He didn’t understand what “clucking” meant, but he knew he had to find his mama.
— Thank you! — he chirped, and, wobbling on his thin little legs, he ran toward the door, toward the light, drawn by the sunbeam.

    The barn was dark and cool, but outside — a true wonder! The sun! Flowers! Trees! Everything sparkled, moved, and smelled of spring. The chick froze on the threshold, spread his tiny wings, and his fluffy feathers quickly dried in the warm rays.
— Wow! — he whispered in awe. — How beautiful!

    The sun played with its rays, peeking out from behind fluffy white clouds. Could it be… my mama? So big, so bright, so warm…

    He took his first step on the soft grass, then another… and ran, joyfully hopping like a feather in the wind — toward the sun, toward adventures — and, of course, toward his mama.

    And Spider Boris stayed in his corner, swaying on a silken thread and muttering quietly:
— Quick little one, ran off! Well, let him. As long as he doesn’t get lost…

Chapter 2. Chick and Daisy Rita

    Chick was happily running along a forest path, his tiny feet pattering through the grass. He had just hatched, and everything around him was new, bright, and amazing! The sun peeked out, then hid behind a cloud, and Peep tried to chase it, thinking maybe it was his mama.

    But suddenly — right in front of him — stood a snow-white flower with a bright yellow centre, almost like his own little head.
— Oh! — Peep chirped. — Maybe this is my mama?

    He stepped closer, lifted his beak politely, and asked: — Hello! Are you, by any chance, my mama? The flower swayed gently in the breeze and laughed brightly:
— Oh, sweet little one! Of course not. I’m far too young to have children. I still have lots of blooming to do! Flowers have children when we begin to fade — that’s when the wind carries our seeds. But I’m in full bloom! Just look how fresh and white I am, with a perfect yellow center!

    She adjusted a petal with flair and added:
— My name is Daisy Rita. And, by the way, I’m very beautiful. Don’t you think?

    Chick blinked: — Yes… you really are very beautiful. I’m sorry, I just… I’m looking for my mama.
— Well, you’re clearly not a flower, — said Rita. — You’re a chick. And your mama is a hen. Didn’t you know that? What’s your name?

    Peep thought hard. He truly didn’t know who he was. He had only just hatched! He scratched behind his ear with a wing and chirped: — Peep-peep-peep…
— Peep! What a lovely name! Chick Peep! So nice to meet you, — said Rita. — You know, I have many admirers. Everyone adores my beauty and falls in love with me at first sight!

    Chick Peep didn’t quite understand what “admirers” meant, but he nodded politely. — When you grow up, maybe you’ll become one of my admirers too, — Rita continued.
— But for now, you’re just a baby. And honestly, you shouldn’t be wandering so far from home all alone.
— Yes, you’re right, ma’am, — said Peep, proudly accepting his name.
— Oh, look, Peep! — Rita suddenly exclaimed. — Here come my friends — Zhuzha and Mila! Wait, I’ll introduce you as my new admirer. They’ll be thrilled and spread the news across the meadow!

    Peep froze, lifting his beak to the sky. And then — bzzzzzz! — two cheerful bees flew in.
— Rita! — shouted Zhuzha. — Congratulations! A new suitor?

    Without hesitation, she landed right on Peep’s yellow head, thinking he was a new flower. — Ouch! — Peep chirped, startled. Zhuzha accidentally stung him.
— Ow-ow-ow! That hurts! — cried Peep, and without knowing where to go, he dashed away, flapping his wings and stumbling through the grass.

Chapter 3.🐣 Will you be my friend?

      🐥💦Chick Peep was running along the path so fast that his little feet could barely keep up with his curiosity. He glanced around, remembered the bee sting, and thought that finding a mama was no easy task. And suddenly — oh! The ground disappeared beneath him, and Peep tumbled down like a fluffy ball.
— Splash! — echoed loudly, and Peep landed right in a pond.

    Cold water soaked him from head to toe. He surfaced, flapped his wings, scrambled to the shore, and began shaking himself off like a soggy toy.
— Bravo! What a jump! — came a cheerful laugh.

    Peep froze. Who was that?

    He squinted and saw, sitting on a lily pad, a green frog with sparkling eyes and a wide smile.
— You’re so funny! — she said. — Who are you? And what are you doing in my pond?
— I… I’m Chick Peep, — he chirped softly. — I’m looking for my mama. I fell into the pond by accident…

— Well, it happens! — laughed the frog. — I’m Laura. Frog Laura! Why do you need a mama? Let’s be friends instead!
— Friends? — Peep repeated. — What does that mean?
— Oh, how can you not know! — Laura bounced. — Being friends means jumping off lily pads together, splashing water, catching mosquitoes, diving to the very bottom!
— Oh… — Peep shivered. — I don’t know how to dive…

    But Laura wasn’t listening anymore. She leapt so high it looked like she might fly away — and then, splash! — she landed in the water with a noisy burst.

    Peep was soaked again. He trembled, shivered, and quietly thought: — I really don’t like this… Maybe I don’t know how to be a friend…

    He sighed sadly, turned around, and ran away from the pond — wet, cold, and a little bit lonely.

    And Laura, popping her head out of the water, watched him go and called: — Hey, Peep! Where are you going? … Oh no! Another one ran off! I’m just not lucky with friends…

🍄Chapter 4. Chick Peep and Timothy the Toadstool

      Chick Peep wandered down the forest path — wet, tired, and very, very sad. The world, which had felt so bright and magical just moments ago, now seemed enormous, noisy, and a little frightening. He walked slowly, dragging his tiny feet, and sighed softly:
— I’m all alone… No mama, no friends…

    He was cold, hungry, and lost. He didn’t know where to go. And then, at the edge of a clearing, he saw a large, bright mushroom with a red cap dotted in white — it was Timothy the Toadstool. Peep crawled under the wide cap, curled into a ball, and quietly cried. So quietly… that not even the sky could hear.
— Hello there, little one. What’s wrong? Why are you crying? — came a gentle, thoughtful voice.

— Hello… Peep peep peep… I’m crying because my life didn’t work out. I’m very unhappy.
— That’s sad. You’re so small. How could life go wrong already?
— I got lost, stung by a bee, fell into a pond and nearly drowned, got soaked, got cold, got hungry, and now I don’t know where I am…

    Timothy gently tilted his cap and began to speak, as if thinking aloud:
— Oh dear… you’ve had quite a day. But it will pass. Everything passes. Everything changes. And then it circles back again. Life has joy and sorrow, like day and night. If it’s night now — morning will come. And then night again. And morning again. What was, will be. I’m not sure how to help you, but I do know one thing: don’t drink toadstool tea — better to shift how you see things.

    Peep listened carefully, though much of it sounded strange and unusual. He didn’t understand the words, but he felt that Timothy was speaking about something important. Something grown-up. This wasn’t just a mushroom — this was a forest philosopher. And a rather peculiar one — not quite of this world.

— Peep peep peep… I’m sorry, but I really don’t understand… — whispered Peep.
— I’ll explain, of course! — Timothy brightened. — Think of it this way: you fell into a pond and got wet — but you climbed out, and soon the sun will dry you. That’s better than falling into the endless ocean.
— You say you have no friends — but you have time. Time to discover what kind of friends you’re looking for, and why you need them. A true friend isn’t just someone nearby. A true friend sees you, understands you, accepts you. And to be understood — you must first understand yourself.
— You say your life didn’t work out… Oh dear, where did you hear that phrase? Think — what would old rooster Karl say, the one farmer’s wife Aunt Polly turned into soup yesterday?
— And yes, the bee sting hurts — but it will heal. Be glad it wasn’t a crocodile. Being stung is unpleasant, I agree. In fact, I know quite a bit about bee venom. Would you like to hear about its effects on tissue and the nervous system? I’m quite the expert. Many say I’m poisonous, but that’s not entirely true. The alkaloids I contain affect…

— Peep… peep… I’m just little. I need my mama. I’m looking for her… — Peep interrupted, his voice trembling.
— Bravo, little one! Now allow me to disagree. You’re a rare lucky soul! You have no idea how fortunate you are. Because you know who you are. And even more — you know what you want. So many creatures live their whole lives without ever asking that. But you — so tiny — and already you know. That’s wonderful!
— And you’ve touched on something very important. I’ve pondered it for many days. I’d love to share my thoughts on parent-child connection. Of course, I must admit — I’ve never raised children myself, but that’s beside the point.
— A mama… Yes, a child needs a mama — like a tree needs roots. Without her, it’s hard to grow. Every little one needs warmth, care, love. Someone to hug when it’s scary. Someone to explain when it’s confusing. A mother’s love is like sunlight — it warms you, even when you’re alone. Like rain for a seed — without it, you won’t grow.
— You haven’t found your mama yet, — Timothy continued, — but I believe you will. You’ll find her because you know what you’re looking for. And that — that is the most important thing.

    Timothy was delighted to have a listener and had no intention of stopping. He spoke softly, thoughtfully, like a lullaby. His voice was calm, hushed — like the forest whispering at sunset. Peep began to nod, then yawned, curled up under the mushroom cap, and gently fell asleep, cradled by Timothy’s quiet murmuring.

    Timothy noticed, but wasn’t upset. He looked at Peep and, smiling faintly, whispered:
— Asleep already. That’s good. Sleep, little one. Morning is wiser than evening. Still… allow me to share a few final thoughts on the nature of love and happiness…

    And he continued his monologue, mumbling gently to himself, as if weaving an invisible forest tapestry — each thread a thought, each bead a truth, waiting for someone ready to hear.

🦉🍵 Chapter 5. Welcome to Owl’s Nest

      Day quietly slipped away, hiding in the shadow of the old oak. Soon, the soft rustle of wings was heard in the forest. Sophie the Owl, wise and kind, arrived. She was an old friend of Timothy’s, and he often sought her advice — especially when it came to simple, everyday matters in which he wasn’t particularly strong.
— Good day, Timothy! Talking to yourself again, are you?
— Dearest Sophie, as always — perfectly timed. Look at this clever little one who came to listen to my thoughts. He’s remarkably wise for his age. And you shouldn’t mock me so lightly. I merely have the habit of speaking my thoughts aloud.
— Oh, he’s so tiny… — whispered Sophie, gazing tenderly at the sleeping Peep. — And all alone. Looks like he’s lost. What he needs is a mama, not your tangled philosophies.
— Undoubtedly! That’s precisely the philosophical conclusion we reached together.

    She gently tucked the chick under her wing and carried him to her cosy nest, hidden in the hollow of an old oak tree. It smelled of dried herbs, honey, and… pastries made from dried bugs — Sophie baked them using a special recipe.

    Her nest was pure magic: soft pillows, crocheted rugs, owl-patterned napkins, cobweb curtains, and even tiny slippers for guests. Sophie adored handicrafts and made everything herself — with love and patience.

    Peep woke up, stretched, and looked around in surprise. — Where am I?..
— You’re my guest, little one, — smiled Sophie. — I’m Owl Sophie. And you, as I’ve heard, are Chick Peep. Would you like some tea?
— Yes… — whispered Peep. — Thank you…

    Soon they were sitting at a tiny table, sipping fragrant herbal tea and nibbling pastries. Peep ate carefully but with delight — they were tasty, though a bit unusual.
— How did you end up alone in the forest? — asked Sophie.
— I went to look for my mama and got lost. Now I’m all alone. I have no mama, no friends…
— Well, I’ll help you find your mama. You’re a chick, so your mama is a hen. She lives in the poultry yard. But friends — you choose them yourself. Do you know what a true friend is?

    Peep thought for a moment. He remembered Rita, Zhu-Zhu, Laura… and gently shook his head. — A friend… is someone who doesn’t bite or splash?

    Sophie laughed: — That’s a good start. But a friend is someone who’s there when you’re sad. Someone who listens, helps, doesn’t laugh at you — but laughs with you. Someone who doesn’t leave you, even if you’re wet, scared, or don’t know how to dive.
— How do you become friends? — asked Peep.
— First — you meet, — said Sophie. — Then — you get to know each other. And then — you choose. You choose those who feel close, who understand you, who you trust, who make you feel good. But most of all — it’s your willingness to be there, to help, to care, to share, and to do for your friend what you’d want them to do for you. And if you know how to be a friend — you’ll surely find those who become your friends.

    Peep listened with wide eyes. He felt these words were important. Like the pastries: strange at first, but then — sweet and needed.
— Thank you, Sophie, — he said. — I want to find my mama. And I want to learn how to be a good friend and find real friends.

    Sophie wrapped him in her wing: — You’re already on the right path, little one. And now — it’s time to go home. I’ll show you the way to the poultry yard.

💔 Chapter 6. Panic in the Chicken Yard

    🐔🐣🕷️It was a sunny, lively day in the chicken yard. Life was flowing as usual. In the barn, nestled among straw and eggshells, six fluffy, cheerful chicks had hatched. Six tiny yellow puffballs chirped and stretched their beaks toward Mama Hen. Barbara, though chatty and a bit scatterbrained, was overjoyed — her babies were peeping, running, pecking at grains, and tumbling sideways in the cutest way.

— Oh, what a joy! — she clucked. — My precious, long-awaited babies! What sweet little darlings! I’m such a good mama!

    She carefully climbed into her nest and covered the chicks with her wings. But suddenly, she froze. A strange feeling crept into her heart. She counted the eggshells — there were seven. But only six chicks.
— One… two… three… four… five… six… Where’s the seventh?! One chick is missing!

    Barbara began clucking loudly. She ran around the barn, peeking into every corner. Her six chicks followed her obediently, sensing the worry in her voice and growing frightened themselves.

    She flapped her wings and dashed across the yard.
— Cluck-cluck-cluck! My baby! Where are you? Answer me!

    The hens gathered near the feeder and exchanged glances.
— There she goes again, — one muttered. — Six chicks hatched, and she’s still fussing. It’s never enough for her.
— Looks like she lost one, — said another. — Probably a fox or a hawk snatched it while she was gossiping. She talks more than she broods.

    But Barbara didn’t hear them. She searched under logs, inside feeders, even in the water bucket.
— Cluck-cluck-cluck! Baby, please answer!

    Finally, she rushed into the barn, where Spider Boris sat in his usual corner, thoughtfully fiddling with his web.
— Boris! — she cried. — My baby is missing! Have you seen him?
— How would I know? I’m not your chicks’ nanny. I’ve got my own business.
— But he did hatch, didn’t he?
— He did. But where were you? Chatting with your girlfriends in the yard? Go ask them. I told you — stay with the nest, don’t run around. Idle gossip never ends well. But you just kept clucking…
— I only stepped out for a minute to peck some grains…
— Your minute lasted over an hour. Don’t fool me. Be grateful for what you have. Six chicks — that’s plenty. So much noise and peeping.
— But he’s my child! He’s lost! He might be in danger! I have to save him! — Barbara sobbed. — I love him!
— Then un-love him.
— That’s impossible. I can’t stop loving him. You don’t understand love!
— Me? Not understand love? I love flies! Especially fat ones with ketchup for breakfast.
— We’re talking about something else entirely. I love him because I want to care for him and protect him!
— If you wanted that, you wouldn’t have lost him. You’re just worried the other hens will think you’re a bad mother.

— You don’t understand maternal love! — Barbara cried, still searching. — A fox could’ve eaten him, or a hawk could’ve taken him. Yes, you’re right — I’m a bad mother, — she whispered, lowering her head.

    She knew Boris was right and felt deeply ashamed.
— I’m so sorry this happened, — she sobbed. — You’re cruel!

    Boris didn’t like Barbara. But seeing her grief, her despair, the fear and sorrow in her eyes — he softened.
— Don’t cry. No fox ate him.

    He sighed, climbed down his web, and looked her in the eye.
— Alright. Don’t panic. He hatched safely. I saw him. He ran down the path — toward the sunny meadow. Looking for you.
— Looking for me?.. — Barbara whispered. — He’s all alone…
— He’s clever, sweet, and kind. He even looks like you, — Boris said. — Only smarter. And less chatty.

    Barbara smiled through her tears.
— Thank you, Boris…

    She wanted to kiss him, but he quickly hid in a crack, afraid of being pecked.

    Meanwhile, the hens kept gossiping:
— She lost her chick. Shameful. Negligent mother!
— Definitely not a role model.

    But by the pond, Goose Clara stood quietly. She walked over to the heartbroken Barbara, placed a wing on her shoulder, and said softly:

   
— I understand you. I feel your pain. I have no words to comfort you, but I know your sorrow. I once lost my gosling too. I was young and inexperienced. A hawk took him — right before my eyes. I tried to save him… but I couldn’t.

    Barbara froze. For the first time, she saw not strangeness in Clara’s eyes, but pain. Real, deep pain.

   
— Thank you, Clara, — she whispered. — I’ll find him. I must. I believe he’s alive. He’ll come back.

    And they hugged each other and cried. Bitterly, but together.
— You’re needed by your chicks, — Clara said. — And most importantly — you still have hope. Don’t lose it.

Chapter 7. 🐣Peep’s Return🐔🌞

    🦉Owl Sophie spread her wings and gently carried Peep over the forest. He sat on her back, holding onto her soft feathers, gazing down at the paths, meadows, and pond — all the familiar places he had visited. But now, he wasn’t afraid. He knew: someone was waiting for him.

— We’re almost there, little one, — said Sophie. — The chicken yard is just ahead. The yard was bustling. Hen Barbara ran back and forth, clucking, peeking under bushes, into feeders, even inside an old watering can.

— My baby! Where are you?! — she cried, her voice trembling. Goose Clara stood nearby, silent, but with worry in her eyes. She knew what it meant — to wait and to fear.

    And then — a shadow passed over the yard, a soft flap of wings, and Owl Sophie landed gracefully right in front of Barbara. On her back sat Peep — a little ruffled, but glowing with joy.
— Mama! — he chirped.
— My baby! — shouted Barbara, rushing to him, wrapping him in her wings, pressing him close, kissing the top of his head. — My little darling! My firstborn! My clever, brave sonny!

    Sophie smiled, adjusted her glasses, and said:
— He tried so hard. And he understood so much. When he grows up, he’ll be the wisest of all.

    Clara stepped forward, embraced Peep with her big wing, and whispered:
— You came back. That means everything will be alright.

    The three — Barbara, Clara, and Sophie — hugged Peep, marveling at his courage and thoughtfulness, and even shed a few tears of happiness. And Peep looked at them and thought:
— There they are. True friends. And Mama. The best mama. Soon his brothers and sisters came running — six fluffy, curious little chicks.

    Sophie smiled and said gently:
— This is your brother. His name is Peep.

— Peep! Peep! Where were you? What did you see?
— I was looking for Mama, — he said. — And I found her. And friends too.

    And together they ran across the yard, chirping joyfully, hopping over blades of grass, chasing sunbeams.

    And Owl Sophie, perched on a branch, watched them and whispered softly:
— When the heart is open — the path always appears.

🌟 Questions and tasks for the young reader

1. What is this chapter about? Tell the story in 3–5 sentences.

2. Who is the main character in this chapter? Describe them: appearance, personality, and mood.

3. Choose one scene you remember best. Draw it, and add small details you think are important.

4. If you could ask the main character one question, what would it be? Write it down — and try to answer it as the character.
