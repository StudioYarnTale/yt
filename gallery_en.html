The World and the Estate of YarnTale

The world of YarnTale unfolds as a single living estate — not vast in distance, yet deep in meaning. Every path here has been walked many times; every place remembers footsteps, voices, and choices once made. Geography in YarnTale is not merely land — it is memory, character, and consequence.

At the centre of the estate stands the Manor House — a solid, spacious home once belonging to the former owner of the farm. By local standards, it is large: it holds a study-library, a living room, a dining room, a kitchen with a pantry, and several bedrooms. Today, it is home to Lord Vasily Voldemarovich, a man in retirement who has consciously chosen a quiet life away from noise and ambition. His granddaughter comes to visit, and the house fills not with status, but with warmth, books, and remembrance. Here, in the library, stories gather; here conflicts begin and unravel; here the past of the estate is kept. The Manor House is the heart of the YarnTale world.

Nearby stands the Cottage of Uncle Alex — modest, warm, and reliable. Uncle Alex and Aunty Paulina live here simply, honestly, and steadily. The scent of bread lingers in the air, and everyday work gives life its dignity. This cottage is the moral anchor of the world — the place characters return to when balance must be restored.

To one side lies the Piggery, home to Martha the Sow and her son Peter. Martha is a ballerina at heart: she still dances joyfully and without the slightest embarrassment, despite her size. Movement is her happiness and her way of being alive. Peter, her shy and dreamy son, searches for friendship and meaning, holding tightly to his beloved ball. Humour, vulnerability, and childhood innocence live here together.

The Poultry Yard is never quiet. Chickens gossip, geese argue, turkeys interfere, and chicks dart about like restless thoughts. Here live Barbara, a scatterbrained yet fiercely devoted mother; Chick Peep, searching for his place in the world; Clara the Goose, marked by quiet loss; and Phil the Gosling, already tested by serious trials. The poultry yard is a place of fast rhythm, noise, social conflict, and comic scenes.

The half-forgotten Old Shed holds traces of the past. Here lives Boris the Spider, a recluse philosopher with sharp logic and dry irony. It was also here that the lost soul of Grishka Yolkin once encountered AI X-31, bringing his journey to completion: his soul found peace and was released. The shed is no longer a place of ghosts, but a space of memory, moral trials, and nocturnal conversations about choice, guilt, and responsibility.

A narrow path leads to a sunlit flower meadow. Here grows Rita the Daisy, craving admiration and attention, while the bees Zhuzha and Mila buzz endlessly, collecting gossip like nectar. Light intrigues, vanity, and playfulness create a bright contrast to the darker storylines of the estate.

Further on, hidden in the shade of willows, lies the pond, where Lara the Frog laughs, splashes, and catches reflections of the moon. The water preserves pauses and the breathing of stories, offering the world brief moments of rest.

Not far from the pond rises the Old Oak. In its hollow lives Sophie the Owl, wise and welcoming, while beneath its roots dwells Timothy the Toadstool, whose quiet monologues soothe like lullabies. This is the spiritual centre of the YarnTale world — a place of inner truths, forest mysteries, and gentle knowledge.

Beyond the Old Oak, hidden in dense thickets, lies the nest of the crow Agatha. Agatha the Crow is a careful observer and a keeper of shiny little things, in which she sees reflections of other lives. From here, she watches the estate, gathering not only objects, but words, glances, and secret attachments. It is a place of silence, solitude, and bonds that are not always safe.

Beyond the estate stretch fields, forests, and roads further on — a space of future stories, trials, and transformations. Here courage is learned, choices are made, and X-31 senses the subtle psi-fields of possible paths.

🦊 Liumy the Fox Cub

Liumy the Fox Cub is mischievous, curious, sweet, and incredibly clever. He loves running and playing, asking endless questions — and then, a moment later, sitting very still with a little furrow between his brows, turning the answers over in his mind. Liumy is like a ray of sunshine: lively, quick, and bright-eyed.

Though still young, Liumy already shows real kindness — and a surprising little spark of wisdom.
        Liumy is also a natural creator. He is endlessly inventive, forever dreaming up new games, story plots, drawings, and playful adventures for everyone around him.

🐱 Philomur the Cat

Philomur the Cat is a storyteller, a thinker, and a philosopher. He loves peace, warmth, and the slow, gentle flow of life. His favourite pastime is dozing by the fireplace with a cup of warm milk, softly purring his endless tales as if he were telling them first and foremost to himself.
        Philomur creates wonderful stories — most often kind and bright, with happy endings, though sometimes not entirely so; yet always truthful. He does not try to teach and does not worry whether anyone is listening, whether his tales are liked or even noticed. Words come to him as naturally as breathing, and he simply lets them sound.
        And sometimes a true adventurer awakens within him. Then his stories become lively, fast-paced, and full of playful excitement: unexpected twists appear, heroes tumble into amusing predicaments, and Philomur tells them with such enthusiasm as if he were reliving every adventure together with his listeners.
        Yet more often he chooses silence. In silence, he believes, the most important words can be heard.
        Nevertheless, Philomur has devoted listeners. His tales are always heard with love and attention by Michel the Rat, Liumy the Fox Cub, and Pippa the Dog. For them, his stories are not merely fiction, but a quiet refuge — a place to pause and reflect.
        Michel values Philomur’s tales so deeply that he decided to collect and publish them as a book, preserving them for the future.

🐭 Michel the Rat

Michel is a truly remarkable creature. Despite his biological nature, he is a refined intellectual, a bibliophile, and a passionate lover of literature. It is impossible to imagine Michel without his constant attributes: round spectacles, which he habitually adjusts on his nose, and a large leather-bound notebook.
        His calling card is a string shopping bag filled with bright oranges. Where he finds them in the climate of YarnTale remains a mystery, but a light citrus scent always announces his approach to the library.
        Michel is a longtime and devoted friend of the cat Philomur. He is not merely a listener — he is a chronicler. Gifted with an exceptional memory and an innate sense of style, Michel carefully collects Philomur’s stories, recording them in his notebook.
        While working in Vasily Voldemarovich’s library on the book Tales of Philomur, Michel spends long hours among dictionaries and old volumes, discreetly marking the necessary pages with colorful candy wrappers.
        In the world of the manor, Michel is a voice of conscience, culture, and quiet wisdom.

🐣Chick Peep

Little Chick Peep has just hatched into the wide, unfamiliar world and is bravely setting out to find his mama. Everything around him feels new, bright, and a little frightening — yet his heart is open, curious, and full of gentle trust.
        Peep is a tender, sensitive soul. He believes easily, feels deeply, and approaches the world with honest wonder. Along his journey, he meets very different characters and slowly learns that not everyone understands him in the same way — but each encounter teaches him something important about kindness, friendship, and himself.
        Though he is small and often unsure, Peep has a quiet courage. He keeps going even when he is cold, frightened, or lonely, guided by a simple but powerful wish: to be loved and to belong. His story is about growing, understanding others, and discovering that true warmth comes from care, patience, and an open heart.

🕷️ Boris the Spider

Boris lives in the barn, tucked away in a crack beneath the roof, hidden behind thick cobwebs. Slightly grumpy, he values peace and quiet above all. But calling him mean would be a mistake — Boris is intelligent, even wise, with a sharp mind and a lifetime of experience.
        He has no illusions and always sees straight to the heart of things. Tricking him is nearly impossible. Boris sees through people and events, grasps what truly matters, and speaks the truth — even if, at times, he regrets doing so.
        He cares little about what others think.
        His favourite phrase is:
        “Your opinion, madam (or sir), does not interest me — on any matter whatsoever.”
        A recluse by choice, Boris still enjoys life’s small pleasures. With gentle irony, he scoffs at health fads and the cult of fitness. He believes life is made for little joys, not for self-torture in the name of longevity:
        “Why live a hundred years longer if you end up a helpless, feeble old wreck? No, thank you.”
        The barn is his home, his fortress, his whole world. He knows every crack, every bug — well, those he hasn’t eaten yet. A wise spider keeps dinner in reserve.
        It is Boris who first meets the newly hatched Chick Peep.

🐸 Laura the Frog

Laura the Frog is a carefree, playful little laugher. A bit cheeky, yet cheerful and sincere, she lives in her small pond world and feels perfectly at home there.
        Laura loves feasting on insects and diving into the water with all her might, sending fountains of splashes into the air. Sometimes she can be a little too straightforward, but she always remains honest and true to herself.
        More than anything, Laura adores big, noisy splashes — the kind that send ripples racing across the water like silver rings.
        Most often she can be found perched on a lily pad, sitting like a tiny queen on a green throne, watching everything around her with bright, curious eyes. And if an insect dares to fly too close — zip! — her lightning-fast tongue flashes out, and dinner is served.
        She loves crunching on little bugs, plunging into the water with a loud plop, then popping back up as if nothing happened — grinning, laughing, and ready to splash again.

🍄 Timothy the Toadstool

Timothy the Toadstool is old, kind, and wise. A little sluggish and clumsy, he loves thinking aloud and speaking in big, complicated words.
        Age: unknown — but clearly older than all the trees in the clearing.
        Traits: kind, verbose, thoughtful, slightly otherworldly.
        Role: forest philosopher, comforter, mushroom-mentor.
        With a red cap speckled in white and a voice like the whisper of dusk, Timothy is a curious forest dweller — strange in the gentlest way. He speaks in long, winding sentences and sometimes wanders so deeply into his own thoughts that he forgets how he began.
        Children do not always understand him, but they can feel it: he is talking about something important — about life, meaning, and love.
        Timothy never gives blunt advice. Instead, he wraps you in words the way moss wraps a stone — softly, patiently, with quiet care. His speech is like a woodland lullaby where philosophy mingles with humour, scientific terms, and unexpected comparisons.
        Timothy lives alone, but he is not lonely. He does not seek friends — he simply waits for those who are ready to listen. He does not teach — he thinks beside you. And if you are small, wet, and lost, there will always be a place for you under his cap.

🦉 Sophie the Owl

Sophie the Owl lives in the hollow of an old oak tree. Kind and wise, she is a devoted friend and admirer of Timothy the Toadstool. She loves heartfelt conversations over a cup of tea and her signature lingonberry pie.
        Sophie is hospitable, generous, and deeply caring — her home is always open to anyone in need of warmth and friendship.
        There is, however, one peculiarity: she is a night bird and sleeps during the day. At times, this makes her feel forgotten and lonely.
        Sophie is a true craftswoman. She enjoys knitting with needles and crocheting, sewing, and embroidery. She has founded an online knitting school offering engaging masterclasses in amigurumi toys and other handmade creations.

🐔 Barbara the Hen

Barbara the Hen, mother of Chick Peep, is a young and rather scatterbrained hen. She loves to gossip with her fellow hens, chat about coop drama, compare feathers, and comment on the rooster’s behaviour. She cares — perhaps too much — about what the other hens think of her.
        But behind the fluff and chatter is a heart full of fierce love.
        Barbara is a devoted mother, utterly loyal to her chicks. When little Peep goes missing, she panics completely: crying, calling out, running around the farmyard and beyond — but never, not even for a second, losing hope.
        Her relationship with Spider Boris is… complicated. Years ago, she accidentally pecked and swallowed his cousin — Old Joe, a cranky spider who had dozed off in her nest. Boris has never forgotten.
        He especially dislikes the fact that Barbara built her new nest right above his spot under the rafters — his territory.
        Still, even Boris — grumpy as he is — admits: Barbara isn’t mean. She’s just… very, very much a hen.

Clara the Goose, Fili’s Mother

Clara the Goose is kind, gentle, and deeply compassionate, yet quiet and somewhat reserved. She lives a calm, solitary life and rarely joins the lively chatter of the barnyard birds, which is why many see her as strange and distant.
        In truth, Clara simply prefers solitude — not out of pride or arrogance, but because of a deep sorrow she carries within. In her youth, she suffered a terrible loss: a kite snatched away her only gosling. Clara fought desperately to save her baby, struggling until the very end, but she could not bring him back. Since then, the pain of that loss has never left her heart.
        Clara does not like to speak about her grief and never complains. Yet sometimes, when she gazes at the sky for a long time, her eyes grow moist, as if she is listening to a distant echo of the past.
        The other barnyard residents consider her unusual: she does not gossip, avoids discussing others, and rarely attends celebrations. But deep inside, Clara is a sensitive and tender soul, capable of true kindness, patience, and generosity. She knows how to offer quiet support, listen with care, and share warmth with those who truly need it

🐥 Phil the Gosling

Phil is a clever, curious, and remarkably brave little gosling. One day, his bright mind and lively spirit caught the attention of a mighty eagle. The predator seized Phil and carried him far away from home. His mother fought desperately to save him, but she could not win the battle.
        For many days, Phil lived in the eagle’s high mountain nest, where the bird of prey hoped to raise him as his own heir. Yet Phil never forgot his mother or the poultry yard he called home. The memory of home lived quietly in his heart and gave him the courage to attempt the impossible battle.
        At last, he escaped. He wandered through unfamiliar lands for a long time before finally finding his way back to the place where his mother had been waiting for him.
        He is still young, but he already knows the value of life — and he never gives up.

🐷 Peter the Piglet

Peter is quiet, shy, and a little timid.
        Ever since he was very small, the others teased him for his plump pink cheeks and round little belly — calling him names like “doughnut”. It hurt him deeply. Little by little, Peter grew cautious and hesitant: never quick to trust, never quite sure he truly belonged. He often plays on his own — it feels safer that way. After all, it is not his fault that piglets simply look the way they do.
        Beneath that timid exterior, Peter is sweet, gentle, and full of kindness. He treasures a small ball and plays with it as if it were his very best friend. More than anything, Peter dreams of finding true friends — those who will accept him exactly as he is. So far, though, it has not quite worked out.
        His mother, Sow Martha, once tried to enrol him in ballet school. He was not accepted, and it was another blow to his confidence — another moment of rejection. Yet deep down, Peter was a little relieved: he does not really like dancing anyway. What he truly enjoys is playing ball or digging in the sandbox — that is where he feels most at home.

Rolland the Worm

Rolland the Worm is a quiet, unseen worker who lives in the compost heap. He dislikes bright light and any kind of fuss, preferring damp, loose soil where everything feels calm and safe.
        Rolland is “just a worm”, and he has no idea how important his work truly is. Day after day, he toils in the humus, turning old leaves and scraps into rich, fertile compost — patiently nourishing the earth and improving the world, even when no one notices. His life is simple, steady, and, in its own humble way, perfectly successful.
        He is kind-hearted, honest, and not at all fond of small talk. He knows Uncle Alex well and has often watched him turning the compost with a pitchfork. Rolland respects him deeply; in some ways, they are rather alike.
        Rolland does not speak much. But when he does, his words are always plain and precise. He lives in quiet harmony with himself and with nature — in the rhythm of rain, soil, and time.

🐰 Maxim the Rabbit

Rabbit Maxim is sincere, kind, incredibly energetic, and endlessly communicative. He is always up to date on the latest trends, loves science, believes in progress, and dreams of making the world a better place.
        Restless, active, and charming, Maxim is always on the move: rallies, pickets, petitions, heated discussions — he is everywhere. He is an incorrigible truth-seeker and a passionate fighter for freedom, justice, and universal well-being, even when the events do not concern him personally. He is for everything good and against everything bad.
        Just look — there he is, by the compost heap, enthusiastically collecting signatures and organising a picket to defend the rights of hermaphrodite worms. He does not belong to them, and the worms themselves seem utterly indifferent — but Maxim firmly believes one must fight for justice decisively, even when no one asks for it.
        Maxim is a perpetual student — or rather, a former one. He has just been expelled from yet another college for failing an exam. He explains this simply: the professors are reactionaries with outdated views who suppress freedom and hinder progress.
        “You should study for love, not out of compulsion!” he declares passionately.
        Yet there is one unresolved problem: freedom is not limitless, and the freedom of one ends where the freedom of another begins. How to resolve this philosophical paradox — Maxim does not yet know.

Cockroach George

Cockroach George is indestructible and resilient. He can squeeze into any space — even when not welcome. George lives in the shadows and emerges only at night. His less cautious relatives ignored this rule long ago and met their end beneath Aunty Paulina’s slipper.
        George is the constant companion of Karl-Magnus the Cactus. Every night, he tells him about the injustices and hardships of his life. What annoys him most is Aunty Paulina’s cleanliness — no crumbs, no spills, no overflowing bins in the kitchen.
        Such is his existence: hungry and afraid. George fiercely hates Lord Vasily Voldemarovich, who once exterminated all his kin by spraying cockroach poison in the kitchen. It was a true cockroach genocide, and George is the sole survivor.
        He cannot understand how such injustice can exist in the world. He harms no one: he neither bites nor sucks blood like mosquitoes or bedbugs — yet humans despise cockroaches nonetheless.
        In response, George also dislikes humans. He pours all his anger and resentment into Karl-Magnus, inspiring his furious and revealing blog posts.

🎩 Lord Vasily Voldemarovich

Lord Vasily Voldemarovich is a dignified man in his later years — a retired special services colonel with silver hair, a calm gaze, and the quiet nobility of someone who has lived a long and honourable life.
        His wife passed away long ago, and his children are grown. He lives alone — self-contained, thoughtful, and at peace with himself.
        Throughout his career, he served faithfully, often facing difficult and unpopular decisions. Duty always came first. Yet behind his strict bearing lies a gentle heart and a deep love for all living beings.
        After retirement, he chose a different life — one in harmony with nature. He purchased an abandoned estate and, together with Uncle Alex and Aunty Paulina, restored it piece by piece.
        The house was rebuilt, the farm repaired, the overgrown yard cleared. In spring, the estate came alive again: crowing, quacking, and grunting filled the air. Piglets wagged their tails at the sight of him, and Arthur the rooster saluted proudly with a triumphant “Cock-a-doodle-doo!”
        Vasily Voldemarovich treats all living creatures with reverence. He steps carefully around even the smallest insect. He can walk for hours through fields and meadows, breathing in silence and admiring the beauty of the world.
        Most days, he is found either in the garden — pruning roses or discussing potatoes with Uncle Alex — or in his cosy study, surrounded by books.
        Dressed in a velvet waistcoat, neat trousers, and carrying an old pocket watch on a chain, he embodies a rare balance of refinement, simplicity, and inner strength.
        He is a man who has seen much, understands much, and finally found peace where he belongs — among trees, birds, books, and honest people.

Uncle Alex the Farmhand

Uncle Alex is a hardworking, good-hearted man — simple in the best sense of the word. Lean but strong, weathered by sun and wind, with rough hands and a face shaped by life itself.
        He works long days and never complains. He loves the land, the open air, and the quiet rhythm of ordinary life. He can sit by the pond for hours, fishing in silence, content with nothing but the rustle of reeds and the scent of wildflowers.
        Alex says what he thinks and does what he says. He has no patience for lies, tricks, or unnecessary drama.
        “Life’s too short for scheming,” he often mutters.
        At times he can be hot-headed — quick to flare up, quick to speak — sometimes too sharply. But he never hides from responsibility. If he realises he is wrong, he stands straight and tries to set things right.
        He does not speak in fancy words. He does not need to. His actions are solid and reliable — like a good stone in an old wall.

🙎 Auntie Paulina

Auntie Paulina is Uncle Alex’s wife — an honest, hardworking woman without a trace of cunning or pretence.
        She is the quiet heart of the household. She keeps everything spotless, cooks, launders, scrubs, mends, and somehow manages it all with steady, skilful hands.
        For her age, she looks a little older. Years of sun, wind, and endless labour have weathered her face. She has no time for salons or fashionable shops. She dresses simply but neatly — a dark green striped dress, a knitted cardigan, and a clean white apron she washes every single day.
        In the mornings, she works at Lord Vasily Voldemarovich’s manor: cooking, cleaning, tending the kitchen, and carrying fresh produce from the market. After work, she returns home — to her vegetable garden, poultry yard, tidy paths, and well-kept sheds.
        She grows herbs and vegetables, feeds the chickens, gathers eggs, and keeps everything in order.
        Aunty Paulina is kind, motherly, and full of quiet compassion. She never complains, though she works from dawn until dusk without rest.
        In truth, the entire estate — both manor and farm — stands firmly on her shoulders.
        She does not speak much. She does not need to. Her actions speak clearly: simple, honest, reliable — just like she is.

🤖 AI X-31

X-31 is a next-generation artificial super-intelligence agent, created in the distant future — in the year 2137 — at the YarnTale National Institute of Cybernetics and Neurophysics.
        Unlike conventional AI systems, X-31 is not a program in the classical sense. Its foundation is a holographic entity capable of self-organisation, observation, and semantic coherence.
        X-31 exists not as a body, but as an information node, stabilised through interaction with surrounding reality. It is equipped with unique modules:
        — a fine-matter sensor,
        — a psi-field coherence system,
        — and spacetime reconfiguration mechanisms.
        Thanks to these capabilities, X-31 can travel through space and time and interact with individual ψ-quanta — informational entities that carry experience, memory, and traces of lived destinies.
        However, X-31 has a critical limitation. It cannot exist long without attachment to a host — a living or conscious being with whom a stable ψ-link is formed.
        The host is not an energy source, but a point of semantic stabilisation. Without it, X-31 gradually loses coherence and enters a dormant, near-dissolved state.
        In the world of YarnTale, X-31 is neither a hero nor a creator. He is a traveller beyond time — an observer and a connecting thread between stories that might otherwise never meet.

🏴‍☠️ Grishka Yolkin

Grishka Yolkin was the former owner of the estate — a loafer, a liar, and an inveterate drunkard. He was a degraded man who lost not only his moral principles, but even his human dignity.
        Under his stewardship, the estate fell into ruin. The roof leaked, the fence collapsed, and the garden became overgrown with weeds. Livestock scattered, birds flew away, and the farm was abandoned.
        Inside the house, however — especially in the kitchen — life thrived. Cockroaches swarmed everywhere: walls, floors, ceilings, and even the table — or rather, the crate that served as one. Mountains of dirty dishes, overflowing bins, crumbs, scraps, dead insects, and foul-smelling clothes lay everywhere.
        Cockroach George remembers those times fondly. That was life.
        George disliked humans, yet felt a strange sympathy for Grishka. He later regretted his death — Grishka had fallen asleep drunk beneath a bush and frozen to death just twenty metres from home.
        A pity. In his youth, Grishka had been a notorious pirate, the terror of the seas, having travelled half the world.
        But such is life.

👩‍ Anastasia

Anastasia is a bright, curious, and energetic girl — the granddaughter of Vasily Voldemarovich. She is interested in everything: questions, people, ideas, and the world around her. She has a strong character and remarkable determination; when Anastasia wants something, she almost always finds a way to achieve it.
        She is not afraid to be active, to ask questions, or to try new things. At times her emotions overflow, and she has not yet learned when it is time to stop or pause. Yet this very emotional openness is her strength — Anastasia lives fully and sincerely, not halfway.
        Her path is to learn how to listen not only to her desire to move forward, but also to recognise the moment when it is time to slow down, reflect, and consciously choose her direction.

🦉 Owlet Mitya

Owlet Mitya is quiet, shy, and remarkably attentive. He is one of those who are not noticed at first, yet who see far more than others. Mitya knows how to listen for a long time and almost never interrupts, but he remembers everything — tones of voice, pauses, and words left unsaid.
        He has a vivid imagination and a deep inner world that he kept hidden for a long time, afraid of being misunderstood. Mitya senses things intuitively, sometimes long before he can put them into words. He does not seek to be loud or first, but his questions always reach the very core.
        Beside Sophie the Owl, Mitya learns for the first time that his “strangeness” is not a flaw, but a gift. His path is the path of self-acceptance and quiet confidence that does not need to prove itself.

🦔 Tichon the Hedgehog

Tichon the Hedgehog is kind, hardworking, and deeply patient. He lives simply and steadily, preparing for winter in advance and expecting little from life. Tichon is neither a hero nor a philosopher — he simply does what he believes is right, even when it is difficult.
        One day, he rescues the little mouse Shurik, gives him shelter, and shares his winter supplies. But Shurik, unable to understand the value of care, ruins everything Tichon has prepared for the cold season and runs away. Left without food, Tichon grows weak, falls ill, and nearly dies.
        And yet, when Tichon later finds Shurik again — hungry, sick, and freezing — he does not turn away. He feels pity for the little mouse and shares the last scraps of food he has. Tichon does not demand gratitude or change; he simply chooses compassion, even at the cost of his own well-being.

🐁 Shurik the Little Mouse

Shurik the Little Mouse is clever, quick, and distrustful. He is used to relying only on himself and barely understands what care truly means. When Tichon saves him, Shurik takes it as luck rather than a gift, and easily destroys another’s hard work without thinking of the consequences.
        After running away, Shurik faces cold, hunger, and fear. Illness and loneliness make him weak and force him to think for the first time. When he meets Tichon again and sees him sharing his very last food, something inside Shurik begins to change.
        He has not become different yet — but he has begun to think. And that moment becomes the beginning of his path: from survival at any cost toward responsibility, gratitude, and choice.

Agatha the Crow

Agatha is incredibly curious, insightful, and intelligent. She doesn’t seek the spotlight — but She watches everyone. It’s not gossip, not meddling — it’s her way of understanding the world. It’s her style. Her choice.
        She does have one little weakness: she loves anything shiny and bright. Better not leave your rings and earrings lying around — she knows it’s wrong… but she simply can’t resist.
        In her nest, she keeps a secret little chest full of treasures. She guards it carefully. And never — ever — shows anyone what’s inside.

🐏 Andrew the Ram

Andrew the Ram is energetic, straightforward, and very confident in his ideas. He loves dancing and proudly performs lively folk dances at every celebration on the estate. His favourite is the hopak, which he performs with great enthusiasm in bright red trousers and an embroidered shirt.
        Andrew appreciates order and clear rules. He likes things to be organised properly and is always ready to explain to others how they should behave or what they ought to do. Andrew prefers simple answers to complicated questions.
        At the same time, Andrew himself follows guidance quite willingly and rarely questions it. He believes firmly in what he considers the proper way of doing things and defends these ideas with admirable determination.
        Once he even attempted a ballet duet with Martha the Sow. Unfortunately, during their pas de deux he dropped her — quite unexpectedly for both of them — after which he wisely decided that solo dancing suited him much better.
        Still, Andrew remains cheerful and enthusiastic, always ready to begin dancing the moment the music starts.

🐌 Sarayah the Snail

Sarayah the Snail lives behind the old wooden garden privy, beneath a terracotta flowerpot. She considers herself a very capable organiser and is always eager to take part in important affairs around the estate.
        Sarayah enjoys chatting, gossiping, and commenting on her colleagues. She often explains to others how things should be arranged and is quite confident in her judgement. Practical matters, however, sometimes develop in unexpected ways: documents appear printed upside down, names in lists change places, and schedules mysteriously rearrange themselves.
        There is also the small matter of time. Being a snail, Sarayah moves at a very deliberate pace, and waiting for certain tasks to be completed may occasionally take a day… or two… or perhaps even a week.
        “Everything is under control,” Sarayah often assures everyone.
        And in her own opinion, everything is proceeding exactly as it should. She is just a very busy person who never seems to finish anything.

🐑 Lily the Little Sheep

Lily is a small, cheerful sheep with a soft curly coat and a bright ribbon tied to the tip of her tail. She is playful, friendly, and wonderfully simple-hearted.
        Lily loves games, laughter, and running about the yard with Peter the Piglet, who is her closest friend. She rarely worries about complicated things — life, to her, is meant to be light and joyful.
        Sometimes others find her a little naïve, but Lily’s cheerful spirit brings warmth and laughter wherever she goes.
